# Survey of the Group's context-brief tooling and the learning-to-code pipeline

The founder's assignment: a deterministic brief generator. Given a topic ("submodule forest", "Tidepool pool engine", "agent harness") it gathers what the Group holds on it (terms, specifications, rulings and decisions, design documents, notes, code entry points) and emits one bounded Markdown brief a Sonnet-class agent can be handed as context. The founder's name for the thing is the context spacesuit (note of 2026-04-06). The founder also wants the pipeline from learning and context through specification, architecture and design to code to be itself something the Group repairs and improves.

This note records what exists, so the generator extends it. The finding in one sentence: the Group has a dozen partial implementations of parts of the idea and no tool that does it. Every ingredient exists (a topic dossier, a bounded evidence reader, a budgeted ranker, a content-addressed packet format, a quality rubric, a usage journal), the pieces are not connected, and several of the connections that were made were cut by the deletions of 2026-09-22 and 2026-10-08.

## Findings

1. The nearest existing thing to the requested generator is CAIRN's `archive.topic(name)` (`KS/src/archive.mjs`), served as the MCP tool `vault_topic` and the `find-gaps` prompt (`KS/src/mcp-tools.mjs`). It returns a JSON dossier of paths and counts (glossary homes, namesakes, pointing documents, mentioning documents, neighbourhood tallies, gaps with a suggested filing path). It has no CLI command, returns no document text, and is not bounded in bytes.
2. The nearest existing thing to the bounded packer is `readCodingContext` (`CDE/src/coding-context/read-coding-context.mjs`): byte-budgeted, digest-bearing, deterministic, reports every omission with a reason. It runs.
3. The nearest existing thing to the ranker-with-budget is `projectContextRelevance` (`CDE/src/coding-context/context-relevance.mjs`), served as the MCP tool `jsnode_context`. It selects packages, bins, exports, functions and types for a prompt, never documents.
4. The most-used retrieval surface is the JSNode server: 3,930 journalled calls between 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-21, of which 1,528 `jsnode_search`, 407 `jsnode_term`, 226 `jsnode_context`, 147 `jsnode_section`. It covers packages, glossary identities and sections. It does not cover notes, rulings or specifications.
5. The house has already defined what a context packet is and how to judge one: `formContextPacket` and `judgeContextPacket` in the inference engine (`CIE/src/context/`), with five criteria (self-containment, machine-checkable goal conditions, lane boundaries, resolvable citations, output contract). The coding-context packet builder computes the same five as `packetQuality`. A brief emitted in this format needs no adapter at dispatch.
6. The glossary index that would give a brief its terms refuses half the glossary: 4,744 of 9,469 term carriers admitted, 2,982 empty-bodied stubs, and the founder's own `context-spacesuit` term refused (rules `record` and `unknown-key`; its frontmatter carries `date-created`, `relates` and `instance-of`, none of which the term schema's property list admits at the top level). Its identity map keeps one record per identity while 404 identities are carried by more than one section.
7. The retrieval that was meant to feed the Group's agents (the deterministic doctrine suit of `agent-orientation`, the anchored-findings hook, the `jsnode-server` service unit) points at directories and paths that no longer exist, or was removed: the Codex hook wiring (`.codex/hooks.json`) was deleted on 2026-09-22 with the other agent configuration, re-added on 2026-09-29 together with the knowledge hook, and deleted again in the 2026-10-08 snapshot commit.
8. The house theory is complete enough to specify the brief: rulings first and counted by denominator (2026-07-04), bounded and ranked with omissions counted (suit operator), content-addressed and checkable (context packet), model tier falls as the fiber fattens (2026-04-05), repetition as the ranking signal for standing context (2026-09-27), retrieval gated by recall (2026-09-30 Wave 3.4).
9. The seven-stage development language (`dream, plan, architect, spec, tests, docs, code`) exists as code with violation codes and a causal-closure judgment (`communicating/src/language/development/index.mjs`) and has no producer: only the arc42 workbench imports it, and no test exercises it.
10. Specifications are meant to be fabricated from journaled rulings and never handwritten (`SPEC/README.md`). The tree holds 3 `*.specification.json` records against about 218 non-archive specification Markdown files.

## The tools

Each entry: path, what the code does, inputs, outputs, whether it runs.

### A. Topic-keyed readers

#### A1. JSNode server tools: `jsnode_context`, `jsnode_term`, `jsnode_section`, `jsnode_search`, `jsnode_fabrication_records`

- Paths: `CDE/src/analysis/mcp.mjs` (tool declarations and server instructions), `CDE/src/analysis/service.mjs` (held indices and the `mcpDeps` the tools call), `CDE/src/analysis/discovery.mjs` (glossary and section indices), `CDE/src/analysis/journal.mjs` (call journal), deployment record `CDE/content/deployment/systemd-user/workspace-analysis.service` with its `README.md`.
- What it does. A long-running process holds in-memory indices rebuilt on `jsnode_refresh` or on a 60-second interval: the workspace package graph read from `package.json` manifests, the TypeScript/JavaScript declaration index, the glossary index (`buildGlossaryIndex`: a `readdirSync` walk pruned to `content/` glossary directories, one frontmatter parse and one `recognizeTermMarkdown` per carrier), and the section index (`buildSectionIndex`: every `*-services-section` directory with its README first paragraph, children, entries and `package.json`).
  - `jsnode_term {name, includeLegacy}` resolves a glossary term, concept or topic by identity, then frontmatter name, then alias, exact case-insensitive, over `content/text/glossary/{terms,concepts,topics}` and the older `content/glossary/...` shape. Each hit carries path, kind, name, description, aliases, `admitted` and the finding count, `underLegacy`. It returns every match, so a term carried by three sections returns three hits.
  - `jsnode_section {query, limit}` ranks sections by name words and README first paragraph.
  - `jsnode_context {prompt, focus, limit, maxBytes}` is A2 below.
  - `jsnode_search {query, limit, offset, detail}` ranks packages by name, keyword, description and lists source-declared consumers.
  - `jsnode_fabrication_records` lists specification files the JSNode translator accepts and the fabrication receipts joined to packages.
- Inputs and outputs: JSON arguments, JSON results over MCP at `127.0.0.1:18808/mcp` (and HTTP). Every call appends `{at, tool, query, results, truncated, durationMs, error}` to `data/logs/YYYY/MM/DD/jsnode-{server,workspace}-search-journal.jsonl` (committed for 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-21).
- Measured use from those journals (3,930 lines): `jsnode_search` 1,528 (mean 60 ms), `jsnode_package` 782, `jsnode_term` 407 (mean 2.6 s), `jsnode_duplication` 342 (mean 4.3 s, 119 truncated), `jsnode_context` 226 (mean 634 ms), `jsnode_query` 203, `jsnode_section` 147 (mean 1.75 s), `jsnode_duplication_migration` 26 (mean 28 s). The tools were used heavily by agents for 18 days and the journal stops on 2026-09-21.
- Does it run. The code is complete and has tests (`CDE/test/` includes `jsnode-engine.test.mjs`, `jsnode-fabrication-mcp.test.mjs`, `context-relevance.test.mjs`). It needs the pnpm workspace links (`@red-cup-engineering/markdown-engineering-services-section`, `term-engineering-services-section`, `jsnode-package-services-section`, `computer-system-platform` and others). The deployment record is stale in two ways: `ExecStart` runs `src/cli.mjs`, which does not exist (the file is `src/cli/cli.mjs`), and `WorkingDirectory` and `Documentation` name `.../computer-code-engineering-services-section/apps/workspace-analysis`, a path that no longer exists; the README names the unit `jsnode-server.service` and installs a file called `jsnode-server.service` that is committed as `workspace-analysis.service`. Whether the service is installed on Bare Cedar Fog was not observable.
- Verdict: the best-used surface, wrong output shape (JSON, no document text), wrong coverage (no notes, rulings, specifications), a private glossary walker. Keep the tools; stop extending the walker.

#### A2. `projectContextRelevance` and `derive-context-relevance`

- Paths: `CDE/src/coding-context/context-relevance.mjs`, `CDE/scripts/derive-context-relevance.mjs`, test `CDE/test/context-relevance.test.mjs`, export `./coding-context/context-relevance`.
- What it does. Takes an observation of the workspace (`observeJavaScriptContextSurfaces(repoRoot)` via `eachWorkspacePackageDir` and `workspaceTypeIndex` from `@red-cup-engineering/javascript-computer`, or a synthetic observation: the tests pass one in) and a prompt. It extracts at most 16 keywords (identifier-split, lower-cased, de-pluralised, stop-listed), derives a focus package path if the prompt names one, scores functions, types, bin scripts and packages (name 12, detail 5, package 5, keyword 8, focus up to 36), balances results across keywords with a per-keyword quota, then `fitBudget` removes whole lowest-scoring records until the JSON fits `maxBytes` (default 12,000, bounds 512 to 24,000), counting omissions per section. If even the bare record does not fit it returns `coding-context.relevance.refusal` with `output-budget-too-small`.
- Inputs: `{prompt|q|intent, focus, limit (1..48, default 12), maxBytes, metadata}`. Output: `coding-context.relevance` JSON with `completeness {observed, selected, omitted, maxBytes}`.
- Does it run. Has tests; the CLI wrapper uses the house `runJavascriptNodeOperation`. Needs the workspace install for the live observation; the projection function is pure.
- Verdict: the right algorithm for the code-entry-points section of a brief, and the right model for budget behaviour (drop whole records, count omissions, refuse rather than emit a fragment). Reuse as is.

#### A3. CAIRN topic dossier: `archive.topic`, `vault_topic`, `find-gaps`

- Paths: `KS/src/archive.mjs` (`topic`, `readings`, `termConcepts`), `KS/src/markdown-vault.mjs` (the vault: `names`, `search`, `mentions`, `similar`, `questions`, `tellings`), `KS/src/mcp-tools.mjs` (`vault_topic`, `vault_readings`, `vault_mentions`, `vault_questions`, `gardenPrompts().find-gaps`), `KS/scripts/cairn.mjs`.
- What `topic(name, {locale, limit})` does. Folds the name (`nameKey`), finds every document that answers to it (declared title, name, id, alias or file stem) and splits them into homes (path contains `/glossary/`) and namesakes; collects documents whose tellings (frontmatter values and links read as EAVT rows) point at the name or at any answering path; runs a full-text search for documents that mention it and are not already answering; tallies the neighbourhood of all those by `type`, keyword and locale (the section that files them, `localeOf`); finds whether the archive asks the name as an unanswered question; computes readings and pairwise verdicts (`copies`, `same-concept`, `related`, `unconnected`) when several documents answer; and emits gaps `no-home`, `several-homes` (aliases when one locale, registers when several), `copies`, `unlinked-mentions`, `asked`, with `suggestedHome` as a filing path when none exists.
- Inputs: archive options (`root`, optional `region`), a name. Output: `CairnTopic` JSON of paths, titles, counts. No excerpts, no ordering by document kind, no byte bound.
- Does it run. Tests exist for the vault, catalog, MCP tools and vocabulary (`KS/test/`); no test exercises `topic` or `search/`. `scripts/cairn.mjs` opens the vault (`openMarkdownVault`) rather than the archive and has no `topic` or `brief` command: its commands are `summary`, `list`, `search`, `read`, `replace` and `archive search|read|summary|ingest-git|ingest-mirrors|ingest-transcripts`. The MCP route reaches `topic` only through MetroPlat or L-Space. Dependencies: markdown-engineering, signchain, signgraph, eavt, and the code engine. Cost over the whole corpus was not measured: opening enumerates paths (up to 100,000 candidates) and metadata queries observe every admitted document.
- Verdict: the host to extend (see Recommendation).

#### A4. `term-intension` and `path-corpus`

- Paths: `KS/src/search/service.mjs` (`readTermIntension`, verb `term-intension`), `KS/src/search/standing.mjs` (pure FCA settlement, `normalizeTermQuery`), `KS/src/search/path-corpus.mjs` (`readPathCorpus`, verb `path-corpus`), `KS/src/search/concept-resolution.mjs` (journal feed `concept-resolve`), `KS/src/search/schema.mjs`.
- What `readTermIntension({query, root, obsmetrologiaRoot, pathPrefix, limit, includeBody})` does. Normalizes the query into variants (phrase, kebab, snake, camel, Pascal); gathers occurrences from five sources: concept-resolution journal rows, up to 800 Markdown files under the target (frontmatter title/name/alias/tag/heading, optionally body, graded strong/medium/weak), TypeScript module topology if the target is a package, Lean declarations from an Obsmetrologia root, and `readPathCorpus`; closes the occurrences with a formal-concept-analysis context; reads four lenses (physical/material, computing/term, semiotic/intensional, ludic/game); returns status `open | thin | connotation | anchored | polysemous`, anchors, open obligations and a `contextInjection` string (status, up to four anchors, lens statuses, intent attributes, occurrence count).
- `readPathCorpus(root, {query, limit, maxFiles 512, maxBytesPerFile 24 KiB})` walks the file census, retains the top `maxFiles` paths by path-word match in a bounded heap, reads the head of each, and returns per-file term vectors (path 4, tree 2, class 2, heading 5, symbol 5, content 1), role (`orientation`, `research-note`, `record`, `content`, `source-module`, `proof-source`...), headings, symbols, excerpt (420 characters around the first match), matched and missing terms, evidence strings, and corpus concepts.
- Reachability: only through MetroPlat operation families (`apps/metroplat/src/operation-family-loaders.mjs` registers `@red-cup-engineering/metroplat/term-intension` and `.../path-corpus`). No CLI. Many agency skills list `term-intension` and `path-corpus` among their `requires-capabilities` (`science-services-section/lib/agency/skills/research.md`, the `learn-*` skills of math, `coordinate-knowledge-and-technology`). With the node down (2026-10-08) they are unreachable. No test covers them.
- Verdict: `contextInjection` is the closest thing in the tree to a one-paragraph topic brief, produced by a settled FCA closure. `path-corpus` is a second filesystem walker. Fold their selection logic into the brief, expose neither as a separate tool.

#### A5. Glossary index reader

- Paths: `TE/src/glossary-index.mjs`, `TE/scripts/project-set-of-term-records.mjs` (bin `project-set-of-term-records`, `--root --out`), `TE/src/term-carrier.mjs` (`recognizeTermMarkdown`, admission), `TE/apps/term-cli.mjs` (`term index|read|render|recognize|admit|write|write-set`).
- What it does. `findGlossaryTermMarkdownPaths(root)` walks `content/text/glossary/terms/*.md` (excluding `legacy`, `triage`, `node_modules`, `dist`, `test`, `fixtures`). `projectGlossaryIndex({root, documents})` recognizes each carrier, keeps admitted ones as records `{identity, name, description, aliases, keywords, relations, references, provenance, body, path, section, scheme}` sorted by identity then path, byte-identical on re-derivation, and lists refusals separately. `createGlossaryIndexReader(index)` returns `resolve(identity)`, `neighborhood(identity)` (outgoing and incoming carrier-range relations), and `search(query, {limit})` with weights name-exact 100, name 40, alias 25, keyword 15, description 5.
- Defects that matter for a brief: `resolve` and `neighborhood` use `new Map(records by identity)`, so a term carried by several sections keeps only the last record (404 identities are shared); `search` iterates all records and is unaffected. Only `terms/` is read, so `concepts/` and `topics/` carriers (A1 reads them) are absent. Refused carriers never enter the index, and the census shows 4,725 of 9,469 refused.
- Does it run. Pure functions and tests (`TE/test/glossary-index.test.mjs`); the script needs the workspace install.

### B. Evidence packers

#### B1. `readCodingContext` (ran)

- Paths: `CDE/src/coding-context/read-coding-context.mjs`, `CDE/scripts/read-coding-context.mjs` (stdin JSON in, JSON line out), test `CDE/test/read-coding-context.test.mjs`.
- What it does. For `{root, paths, intent, identity, constraints, maxBytes 64,000, maxFiles 64, instructions true, requireComplete false}` it confines every path under `root` (realpath, no escape), prepends the `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md` of every ancestor directory of each requested path when `instructions` is true, opens each file once, reads the requested line interval with fixed-size chunks (binary files refused, source changed during read refused, partial UTF-8 scalars excluded), and returns `evidence[]` entries `{path, role, digest (sha256), digestScope (file|excerpt), bytes, suppliedBytes, byteOffset, omittedBytes, line, endLine, truncated, text}`, `omitted[]` with reasons (`byte-budget`, `file-budget`, `missing`, `binary`, `line-range-outside-source`, `source-changed-during-read`, `complete-artifact-exceeds-budget`), `usedBytes`, `complete`, and `id` (sha256 of the result).
- Run here: copied to a scratch directory with the one external import (`sha256Digest` from the cryptographic-computer-data-engineering package) replaced by a three-line stub; three requested paths and `maxBytes: 300` produced `AGENTS.md` (15 bytes, role `instructions`), `a/spec.md` (40 bytes, whole file), `a/b/big.txt` (245 bytes, `truncated: true`, `digestScope: excerpt`), `missing.md` omitted with reason `missing`, `usedBytes: 300`, `complete: false`, and two runs gave the same `id`. It runs and it is deterministic.
- Limit: reads a filesystem root. For a pinned revision it needs a worktree at that revision (the house opens detached worktrees) or a git-object source.
- Verdict: the packer. Use it for every verbatim excerpt in a brief; its digests make each excerpt checkable.

#### B2. `buildContext` and `buildCodingContextPacket`

- Path: `CDE/src/coding-context/coding-context.mjs` (header comment: "The 'better context beats a smarter model' lever, made first-class and inspectable ... the thing you improve instead of swapping models").
- `buildContext({task, workdir, failures, similarCases})` lists tracked files through the git plan, finds files the task text names (`mentionedPaths`), and reads them with `readCodingContext`. `buildCodingContextPacket({manifest, snapshot, baselineVerification, baselineAcceptance, memories, actionSchema})` builds an `AutonomousCodingContextPacket`: objective, machine checks, acceptance vectors, acceptance authority, lane (territory, focus, writable and readable paths), target artifacts, evidence artifacts with digests, repository map, prior verified knowledge and residue knowledge from trajectory memories, constraints, first moves, output contract, a `packetQuality` object (`selfContainment`, `machineCheckableGoalConditions`, `executableAcceptanceExamples`, `laneBoundaries`, `resolvableCitations`, `outputContract`) and a `resourceAccount`, then a sha256 digest.
- It is mission-keyed (a manifest with a territory, focus paths and an acceptance command), not topic-keyed. Its consumers lived in `AOE/lib/animating/src/becoming/doing/working/software-change/mission/` (`mission-pipeline.mjs`, `trajectory-memory.mjs`, `mission-runtime.mjs` and ten more source modules, with twelve tests: 25 files), which the commit `b006be709b8` deleted; six files remain under that path, and `CDE/README.md` still says "Invoke `aoe-code-mission`". At that commit `buildCodingContextPacket` has no caller I could find.
- Verdict: keep `readCodingContext`; the packet builder is an orphan until the mission pipeline returns. Its `packetQuality` booleans are the machine-checkable half of the judge rubric (B3) and should be lifted into a shared function.

#### B3. Inference context packet and its judge

- Paths: `CIE/src/context/packet.mjs` (`formContextPacket`, profile `org.harmonious-union.context-packet.v1`), `CIE/src/context/packet-carriers.mjs` (request, result, refusal schemas; EAVT projection), `CIE/src/context/judgment.mjs` (`judgeContextPacket`), `CIE/src/context/trade.mjs`, `CIE/src/context/README.md`, `CIE/src/dispatch/context-packet.mjs` (a second `formContextPacket`), `CIE/src/dispatch/context-packet-demand.mjs` (`admitInferenceContext`), `CIE/scripts/context/judge-context-packet.mjs`.
- What it does. A packet is `{objective, createdBy, segments[{ni, mediaType, byteLength, source, compaction?, content}], knowledge[{ni, relation}]}`; every segment is addressed by an RFC 6920 `ni:` sha-256 of its content, duplicate content is refused, every `knowledge` reference must name a segment in the packet, and the packet id is the address of the body. `admitInferenceContext` accepts exactly one of raw `context` or `contextPacket` for an inference work lot, requires the packet to equal its canonical form and its objective to equal the work lot's, and flattens the segments to the supplied context while keeping segment references and knowledge relations as receipts. `judgeContextPacket` sends a packet to a provider and computes the verdict locally from five scored criteria: self-containment, machine-checkable goal conditions, lane boundaries, resolvable citations, output contract (bands: at least 8 strong, at least 4 adequate, otherwise deficient).
- Duplicate: `src/dispatch/context-packet.mjs` (37 lines) and `src/context/packet.mjs` (96 lines) form the same profile twice.
- Does it run. The forming and admission code is pure; judgment calls a provider and was not run. `judge-context-packet` reads a packet on stdin.
- Verdict: this is the wire format and the acceptance rubric for a brief. Emit it; do not invent a fourth packet shape.

## Recommendation

Extend CAIRN's `topic` into `cairn brief`. Add no package, no hook, no registry, no service.

### Why CAIRN

- The Group's own program names CAIRN the owner of agent retrieval (2026-09-30, Wave 3.4: "Its `search` and `fetch` MCP tools are upgraded, not replaced") and its `find-gaps` prompt already asks an agent to run `vault_topic`, `vault_readings` and `vault_mentions` and report. The brief is the deterministic, text-bearing form of that dossier.

### What the brief is

A Markdown document, deterministic for a given revision, topic, flags and corpus, bounded in bytes, with every excerpt verbatim, digest-checked and attributed to a scheme-qualified path. Section order follows T1 and T3: constraints first, because a missing ruling flips the basin.

```
# Brief: <topic>
revision <commit> (clean|dirty) | scope <scheme or whole root> | budget 24000 B, used N | digest sha256:<id of this brief>
coverage: carriers examined E of T in scope; body search on|off
          rulings i/n  specifications i/n  terms i/n  design i/n  notes i/n  code i/n   (n = candidates, i = included)

## 1 Mission            verbatim --mission text, or "none supplied"
## 2 Rulings and decisions   newest first; status line; verbatim lead of each
## 3 Specifications     BCP 14 clauses via specificationReadout, with source line numbers
## 4 Terms              every home (all schemes), admitted|refused(rule)|stub flagged; body lead
## 5 Design and architecture   section README leads, ADR notes, arc42 headings
## 6 Notes              title, date, status, description; ranked
## 7 Code entry points  packages, bins, exports (projectContextRelevance); live anchored findings at those addresses
## 8 Pipeline coverage  stages dream/plan/architect/spec/tests/docs/code present or missing for this topic
## 9 Gaps               no-home, several-homes, copies, stub, refused, body-mention-only, unreadable gitlinks, archive-only
## 10 Omitted           per kind: count dropped, lowest score dropped, the command that reads the rest
```

Rules:

1. Selection is lexical and deterministic. The topic is normalized with `normalizeTermQuery` (phrase, kebab, snake, camel, Pascal variants) and split into keywords as `projectContextRelevance` does. Candidates come from CAIRN's existing metadata ranking (`vault.search(topic, {mode: "metadata"})`, which wraps `searchImmutableRoomIndex`: title, description, aliases, keywords, headings) plus `archive.topic` (homes, namesakes, pointing, mentioning). Ties break by path. No embedding and no model call at assembly time; Wave 3.4 owns semantic retrieval and its recall gate, and the brief upgrades when that lands.
2. Kind comes from the artifact-role directory, as the census does (`carrier-kind-engineering-services-section`'s `./carrier-path`; this repository has it at `repo:services/carrier-kind-engineering-services-section`): `glossary/terms|concepts|topics`, `documents/specifications`, `documents/articles/notes`, `documents/articles/design`, README. Ruling is the one kind with no directory: v0 marks a note a ruling when its `status` contains "operating doctrine", "decision", "ruling" or "accepted", or its filename contains `decision`, `ruling`, `docket` or `adr`. The heuristic is printed in the brief's header so it is visible, and the real repair is a typed `decision` kind (see Pipeline repair).
3. Scope excludes `legacy`, `triage`, `recovered`, `_archive`, `lenticule-import`, `fixtures`, `node_modules` (the census's archive stratum) unless `--include-archive`; archive hits are counted in the gaps line ("archive-only: 14"), never shown. Carriers stating any `consent-status` are excluded, and `visibility` is reported, so a brief never moves a withheld claim.
4. All homes are listed. The glossary reader's identity map is not used; the brief lists every record whose identity, name or alias matches, with its scheme, and flags refused and stub carriers rather than hiding them (a refused carrier still has a body an agent should see; the refusal is a repair item). This implements the 300-answer identity rule of the program's Decision 2.
5. Excerpts are read once, through `readCodingContext`, with `requireComplete: false`, a per-item `--excerpt-bytes` cap, and line ranges chosen by kind (body lead; BCP 14 clause lines; README first paragraph). Each excerpt prints its short digest and `digestScope`, so the reader can tell file from excerpt.
6. Budget is `--budget-bytes` (default 24,000, maximum 32,000; the session's `MAX_MCP_OUTPUT_TOKENS` is 8,000, `jsnode_context` allows 24,000, the standing fiber 12,000). Records are dropped whole, lowest score first, by kind quota (rulings and specifications drop last), exactly as `fitBudget` does; the omitted counts are printed. If the mandatory header, mission and gaps do not fit, the command refuses with `output-budget-too-small` rather than emit a fragment.
7. Denominators are printed (T3). With `--strict` the command exits 1 when any ruling or specification candidate was dropped, so a dispatcher can refuse to send a brief whose doctrine denominator is below 1.0.
8. Provenance is stated in the brief's first lines as `agent-briefing` states it: projected from the records; the records are the authority, not this text.

### How it is delivered

The harness law (T7) forbids a bare, unprimed model prompted fresh. The brief therefore is not a replacement for the office projection; it is the task layer over it: `claude -p --agent <office> < brief.md`, or the brief as the prompt body of an allocated duty on the work rail, or `--format packet` below. It is not written to `.claude/`, not committed, and not registered anywhere.

### CLI shape

```
cairn brief --root <dir> --topic "<words>" \
    [--region <path>] [--mission "<text>"|--mission-file <path>] \
    [--budget-bytes 24000] [--excerpt-bytes 1200] [--per-kind 8] \
    [--code-root <dir>] [--include-archive] [--body-search] \
    [--format md|json|packet] [--strict] [--out <file>]
```

- Output on stdout (or `--out`), diagnostics and obstructions on stderr. Exit 0 normally, 1 with `--strict` and a dropped ruling or specification, 2 on usage errors (the house convention in `cairn.mjs` and `project-agent-briefing.mjs`).
- `--root` is a directory. For a pinned revision, point it at a detached worktree (the house opens them); the brief records `git rev-parse HEAD` and `clean|dirty` from the git codec's porcelain reader. Reading git objects directly (`ls-tree`, `cat-file --batch`, as `census-corpus` does) is the follow-up, not v0.
- `--body-search` adds the full-text mention pass of `archive.topic`; default off keeps the default run cheap, and the coverage line says which was used.
- `--format packet` emits the inference engine's packet: one segment per section (`mediaType: text/markdown`, `source`: the section's paths), `knowledge` relations `constrains` (rulings), `specifies`, `defines` (terms), `describes` (design), `informs` (notes), `implements` (code). It is built with `formContextPacket` and must pass `admitInferenceContext`; the fixture tests assert both, so the same brief can be judged by `judge-context-packet`.
- MCP: `vault_brief` beside `vault_topic` in `KS/src/mcp-tools.mjs`, same arguments; `find-gaps` gains a first step "call vault_brief".

## Pipeline repair: what the gaps feed

The founder wants the pipeline from learning and context to specification, architecture, design and code to be repaired and improved by the Group itself. The survey shows where it is broken and the brief makes it measurable.

- **No stage owns "context for this task".** Learning produces the fiber (C3); the corpus program produces the ledger (F); code produces package indices (A1); nothing produces the per-task layer, so the 2026-10-08 first-mover brief was written by hand at 43 KB. The brief generator is that stage.
- **Rulings are untyped.** They are notes with a free `status`, journal rows, ADR notes. Add `decision` as a carrier kind in `carrier-kind-engineering` (a directory role `documents/articles/decisions/`, a schema with `status`, `date-created`, `supersedes`, `obligations`, `reopens-when`), so the brief reads a kind instead of a heuristic, the ADR rule of T9 becomes checkable, and `SPEC` has a real source for "journaled rulings".
- **Specifications are not fabricated.** 3 of about 218. The brief's section 3 prints, per specification, whether a companion `.specification.json` exists; the census already counts it (`census-specification`). Make that count a ledger measure and ratchet it.
- **The development progression has no producers.** Section 8 of the brief reports the seven stages as present or missing for the topic, using the stage names from `developmentStages`. A later step has `developmentProgressionOf` consume real artifacts; v0 does not call it, because it requires content-addressed artifacts and translation witnesses that do not exist yet. The `missing-stage` codes it already defines are the vocabulary of the report.
- **Gaps are repair work, and the Group has the rail for it.** `no-home` is a wanted page; `several-homes`, `stub` and `refused` are glossary completion (Wave 2.4); `unlinked-mentions` is link repair (Wave 2.1). Emit them as the same `wanted` entries the corpus census writes, so the deriver posts them as signals and no one writes a work item by hand (Decision 13).
- **The brief is the MAPE-K Monitor for tasks.** Record `{topic, revision, digest, sections, omitted, denominators}` as a journal row per brief and join it with `read-demand`: the files an agent reads after receiving a brief are the evidence of what the brief lacked. Rank restated omissions the way the fiber ranks restated directives (T5), and promote a stable ruling into the standing fiber or a check.
- **The measure is the model tier.** T2 says the tier falls as the fiber fattens. The measurable claim: for a fixed task set, the cheapest tier that passes the task's own acceptance command with the brief. Hold that as the Group's metric for the generator, not green tests.
- **Retire the hook, keep the loop.** The founder's order of 2026-09-22 deleting agent configuration and hooks, and the 2026-10-06 decision recorded in the harness projection that sessions carry no hooks, rule out delivery by hook; the Codex hook's second deletion is recorded in the 2026-10-08 snapshot commit. The loop (read-demand, findings, delivery) survives as: demand picks what to anchor, findings are admitted, the brief delivers them.
